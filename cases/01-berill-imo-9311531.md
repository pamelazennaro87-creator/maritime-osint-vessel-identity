# Case 01 – BERILL / IMO 9311531

**Research focus:** Vessel identity reconstruction, ownership/management relationships, historical naming, sanctions-related information and source discrepancies.

## Research Question

How can the identity and historical trajectory of a vessel be reconstructed when its name, flag, management and associated corporate entities change over time?

## Subject

**BERILL** — IMO 9311531

Approximate length: 239 metres  
Approximate deadweight: 105,522 tonnes

### Documented name sequence

STAR LADY → SULU SEA → SL SUCCESS → HAO YU → LEFKADA → AFKADA → REINEL → LINDOR → BERILL

## Investigative Approach

Sources combined:
- Vessel databases
- Maritime records
- Historical vessel information
- Sanctions-related documentation
- Corporate information
- Manager/operator information
- Cross-source comparison

Particular attention was given to relationships involving:
- Gatik Ship Management
- Beacon
- Galena
- Viman Maritime Inc.
- Hei Long Jiang Shipmanagement
- Other associated entities

## Key Analytical Challenge

Different sources did not always present identical ownership, management or operational information. Rather than selecting the most convenient version, discrepancies were treated as an analytical problem.

Questions applied:
- Who is the same entity across different records?
- Which information refers to different periods?
- Which relationships are directly documented?
- Which conclusions require additional corroboration?

## Result

The investigation demonstrated how a vessel can be reconstructed through identity continuity (IMO), historical names, corporate relationships, management records and temporal comparison.

> Conflicting sources are not necessarily a failure of research. They can themselves become an investigative lead.
