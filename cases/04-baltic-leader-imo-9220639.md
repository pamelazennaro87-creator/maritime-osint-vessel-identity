## Documented Activity Relevant to Military Logistics

### C4ADS mapping (public report, November 2026)
C4ADS identified *Baltic Leader* among a set of approximately 12 Russian-flagged (mostly US-sanctioned) vessels assessed as having transported military equipment from Russian Baltic/Arctic ports to West African and Libyan ports for ultimate delivery in support of Africa Corps operations (Mali, Sahel, Libya, etc.).

Key ports highlighted in the broader network:
- Conakry (Guinea) – primary Atlantic gateway toward Mali
- Lomé (Togo)
- Tobruk (Libya)
- Pointe-Noire (Republic of Congo) – emerging hub

*Baltic Leader* is specifically named in the Conakry-related voyage set alongside vessels such as Adler, Siyanie Severa, Patria and Sabetta.

### Earlier public incidents
- February 2022: detained by French authorities in the English Channel (linked to Promsvyazbank sanctions expansion); later released.
- Repeated appearance in open-source tracking of Russian military-affiliated cargo movements toward Africa in 2025–2026.

## Analytical Notes

1. **Role type:** Vehicle/military equipment carrier (Ro-Ro), not an oil shadow-fleet tanker. Part of a parallel logistics network supporting Russian military and paramilitary presence in Africa.
2. **Network pattern:** Multiple sister or co-managed Ro-Ro vessels (Adler, Ascalon, Lady Mariia, etc.) operate under the same or closely related corporate structures and appear on the same Africa-bound routes.
3. **Sanctions gap:** US designations are more comprehensive than UK/EU coverage for several vessels in this cluster, according to C4ADS analysis.
4. **Open questions:** Precise cargo manifests for individual 2025–2026 voyages remain largely inferred from timing, port sequence, subsequent overland convoys filmed in Mali, and pattern analysis rather than publicly released bills of lading.
