# Crewing Agencies & Shadow Fleet Manning

Analytical note based on publicly available investigative reporting (primarily The Insider, July 2026, and supporting open sources). Updated mid-November 2026.

## Why Crewing Matters

While shipowners, managers and charterers of sanctioned tankers often operate through opaque single-ship companies, crewing agencies work more openly. They post job advertisements on Telegram channels and maritime forums, depend on reputation among seafarers, and therefore present a different vulnerability surface.

Without reliable access to qualified masters, officers and ratings, the operational capacity of the shadow fleet is constrained.

## Key Public Findings (The Insider, 2026)

- Analysis of >20,000 posts on popular Telegram seafarer recruitment channels identified **over 1,000 advertisements** seeking crew for vessels that were, or later became, sanctioned as part of the shadow fleet.
- Ads typically omit the vessel name but list **deadweight, year of build and flag** — data almost always sufficient to identify the specific hull.
- Most advertisements appeared before formal designation, but market participants are generally aware of high-risk trades. In some documented cases recruitment continued **after** the vessel had already been sanctioned.

## Operational Characteristics Observed

- Higher advertised pay scales for Master / Chief Engineer roles on certain high-risk tankers (public ads frequently in the $13,000–14,500+ range).
- Mixed-nationality crews common.
- Some masters have been individually designated by Ukraine (GUR publishes names linked to specific vessels and activities).
- Investigative reporting (OCCRP) has separately documented the presence of Russian “supernumeraries” / security personnel on some tankers who can exercise authority over the foreign master and crew.

## Research Implications for OSINT

1. Open Telegram and maritime job boards remain a useful early-warning source: advertisements often precede formal sanctions.
2. Matching DWT + year + flag in an ad against commercial vessel databases can identify the hull before the name is publicly linked.
3. Crewing agencies that continue posting for already-designated vessels become higher-priority monitoring targets.
4. Master-level designations by Ukraine provide a second axis of analysis (vessel + individual).

## Status

This note summarises patterns visible in open investigative sources. Individual company activities and vessel linkages continue to evolve and require ongoing verification against primary lists, AIS data and fresh recruitment monitoring.
