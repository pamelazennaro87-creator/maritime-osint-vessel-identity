# Global Monitoring, Modus Operandi & Captain Recruitment

Evidence-first analytical note based on publicly available reporting, sanctions lists and investigative sources (as of November 2026).

## 1. Global Monitoring Landscape

Multiple independent actors track the shadow fleet. Key public or semi-public sources include:

| Source | Type | Focus |
|--------|------|-------|
| GUR War & Sanctions (Ukraine) | Official public portal | Vessels + Masters; narrative justifications; Ukrainian designations |
| Windward, Kpler, Lloyd’s List Intelligence | Commercial maritime intelligence | AIS behaviour, dark activity, flag changes, cargo estimates |
| CREA / KSE Institute | Think-tank / research | Flag migration statistics, volume estimates, registry pressure |
| US OFAC, EU, UK, Japan | Official sanctions lists | Designated vessels and entities |
| OCCRP, The Insider, Maritime Executive | Investigative journalism | Crewing networks, security personnel, case studies |

**Practical OSINT approach**  
Start with IMO number → cross-check against GUR, Western sanctions lists, commercial vessel databases → examine AIS history for gaps/spoofing → map ownership and management changes → note any master designations.

Ukraine’s GUR portal is particularly useful because it publishes both vessels and a growing list of masters, with explicit statements that masters bear responsibility for AIS manipulation, STS transfers and calls at closed ports.

## 2. Modus Operandi (Observed Patterns)

The operational signature of the shadow fleet is a combination of techniques designed to obscure origin, ownership and movement:

### Identity & Flag Layer
- Rapid name changes after designation or high-risk activity
- Flag hopping between open registries, high-risk African registries, false flags, and increasingly the Russian flag
- Occasional use of “zombie” identities (adopting details of scrapped vessels)

### AIS & Positioning Layer
- **Going dark**: deliberate AIS switch-off during sensitive legs (loading, STS, high-risk transit)
- **Spoofing / GNSS manipulation**: broadcasting false positions (documented clusters near Bosphorus claiming to drift while actually loading at Novorossiysk)
- Identity laundering via MMSI changes alongside flag/name changes

### Cargo Layer
- Ship-to-ship (STS) transfers in international waters to break the cargo trail
- Loading at Russian terminals (Baltic, Black Sea, Far East including Kozmino) followed by delivery to India, China and other third countries
- Occasional mixed histories (Russian oil + Iranian oil sanctions linkage on the same hull)

### Ownership & Insurance Layer
- Single-ship companies in light-touch jurisdictions
- Frequent changes of registered owner and commercial manager
- Limited or non-International Group P&I cover, creating environmental risk for coastal states

### Security Layer (emerging reporting)
Investigative work (OCCRP and partners) has documented the presence of Russian “supernumeraries” / security personnel on some tankers. Public undercover reporting indicates these individuals can exercise authority over the foreign master and crew, monitor communications, and ensure the vessel does not comply with interdiction attempts.

## 3. Recruitment of Captains and Crew

### Public Evidence Base
- Investigative analysis of Telegram recruitment channels (The Insider, 2026) identified over 1,000 job advertisements seeking crew for vessels later or already under sanctions.
- Ads typically omit the vessel name but list deadweight, year of build and flag — sufficient for identification.
- Recruitment continued in some cases even after vessels had been designated.
- Companies and agencies from multiple countries (Kazakhstan, Cyprus, India, Moldova, Georgia, Ukraine, Russia and others) have appeared in public reporting as sources of such advertisements.

### Observed Characteristics
- Higher remuneration is frequently offered compared with non-sanctioned trades (public ads show Master rates in the $13,000–14,500+ range for certain tankers).
- Mixed nationality crews are common.
- Some masters have been individually designated by Ukraine (GUR has published names and linked them to specific vessels and activities). Ukraine has also imposed broader packages targeting hundreds of captains across multiple nationalities.
- Crewing for sanctioned vessels carries elevated personal, legal and safety risk (detention risk, insurance gaps, potential abandonment, possible presence of security personnel with overriding authority).

### Analytical Note on Masters
Ukraine’s public position is that masters possess full knowledge of cargo and ownership and are responsible for AIS manipulation, STS operations and calls at closed ports. Publication of names is explicitly framed as breaking the myth of anonymity in shadow logistics.

## 4. Research Implications

For ongoing OSINT:
1. Monitor both vessel-level and master-level designations (GUR is currently the most transparent public source for masters).
2. Treat crewing advertisements on open channels as potential early indicators of which hulls are being prepared for high-risk trades.
3. Cross-reference any new false-flag or AIS-anomaly case with the recruitment and ownership networks already mapped.
4. Distinguish between verified public reporting and inference when discussing security personnel or control arrangements on board.

## Status

This note summarises patterns visible in open sources as of mid-November 2026. Individual cases and networks continue to evolve and require continuous verification against primary lists and AIS data.
