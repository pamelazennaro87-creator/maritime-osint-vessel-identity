# Flag Change Patterns in the Shadow Fleet (2025–2026)

## Observed Dynamics

Public tracking and investigative reporting show a clear evolution in how sanctioned vessels manage flag status:

### 1. Early phase – Flags of convenience & false flags
After the 2022 oil price cap and EU port bans, many tankers moved to open registries (Panama, Liberia, Marshall Islands) and later to higher-risk or opaque registries (Gabon, Comoros, Gambia, Cameroon, Sierra Leone, Barbados, Palau).

False flags (claiming a country that does not maintain a functioning international ship registry, or for which the vessel is not properly registered) became common. Reported examples include Zimbabwe, Malawi, Benin, Guyana, Guinea, Madagascar and others.

### 2. Pressure on African and small registries (2025)
- EU and UK sanctioned Intershipping Services LLC (UAE), operator linked to Comoros and Gabon registries.
- Several registries (Barbados, Palau) removed or sharply reduced sanctioned vessels.
- Panama significantly cut the number of sanctioned shadow vessels on its books.
- Comoros and Gambia registrations of sanctioned tonnage collapsed after peaks in mid/late 2025.

### 3. Migration to the Russian flag (late 2025 – 2026)
As alternative registries became riskier or closed, a substantial number of already-sanctioned vessels reflagged to Russia.

Key publicly reported figures (various trackers, mid-2026):
- Dozens to over 100 vessels joined the Russian registry in 2025–first half 2026.
- A high percentage of these vessels were already under Western sanctions at the moment of reflagging.
- Many had previously used at least one false flag.
- Russia became one of the leading (and in some datasets the leading) flag of record for the tracked shadow fleet.

### 4. Current dual reality
- A residual population of false-flag or high-risk-registry vessels continues (example: MIDAG claiming Zimbabwe at the time of the November 2026 IRINI boarding).
- A growing share of sanctioned tonnage now sails under the Russian flag, changing the enforcement calculus from “stateless / false flag” interdiction to direct confrontation with a Russian-registered vessel.

## Analytical Implications

Flag hopping is not random noise. It is a predictable response to enforcement pressure:
1. Move to opaque / cooperative registries.
2. When those registries face sanctions or political pressure, shift again.
3. When options narrow, accept the Russian flag (with its own insurance, classification and political protection trade-offs).

Each change of flag, name or manager is therefore an investigative lead rather than a complication to be ignored.
