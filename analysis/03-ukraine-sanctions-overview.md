# Ukrainian Sanctions on Shadow Fleet Vessels

## Primary Public Source

Ukraine’s Main Directorate of Intelligence (GUR) maintains a public “War & Sanctions” portal that lists marine vessels involved in the transportation of sanctioned oil and other restricted activities:

- https://war-sanctions.gur.gov.ua/en/transport/shadow-fleet/

As of the October 2026 update referenced in public reporting, the portal tracked well over a thousand marine vessels in total categories, with hundreds specifically tagged under shadow-fleet / sanctioned-oil transportation.

## Ukrainian Designation Practice

Ukraine has repeatedly imposed national sanctions on shadow-fleet tankers through decrees of the National Security and Defense Council, signed by the President. Publicly reported packages include:

- Dozens of vessels in early 2026 packages (examples of 39 and 91 vessels cited in official communications).
- Ongoing additions throughout 2026.

These designations typically cover vessels that have transported Russian crude or petroleum products from Russian ports (Black Sea, Baltic, Far East including Kozmino) to third countries after the introduction of the G7/EU price-cap regime.

## Relationship to Partner Sanctions

Ukrainian listings frequently overlap with, but are not identical to, US, UK and EU designations. Some vessels appear first on the Ukrainian list; others are added after Western action. Ukraine has stated it shares information with partners to support further synchronization.

## Masters / Captains

The GUR portal also maintains a separate category for masters (captains) associated with the listed vessels. Designation of individuals is less frequent than vessel designation but does occur.

## Research Use

For OSINT purposes the Ukrainian list is valuable because:
1. It is publicly accessible and regularly updated.
2. It often includes narrative justification (specific loading ports, approximate cargo volumes, destination regions).
3. It provides an independent national perspective that can be cross-checked against Western lists and commercial vessel databases.

Any individual vessel research should still begin with the IMO number and then check the full set of designating authorities (US OFAC, EU, UK, Ukraine, Japan, etc.) plus commercial databases for current flag, name and ownership.
